01 / THE AGENCY

Something doesn’t add up.
Good. Start there.

You bought the lifestyle. You learned the lines. You became the target demographic of your own rebellion. Yet the peculiar feeling persists that somebody else has been arranging the furniture inside your ambitions.

The Burroughs Agency investigates missing agency: the capacity to act within—and sometimes change—the conditions of your life. We examine the stories, institutions, incentives, and images that make an arrangement look like destiny.

FORENSIC NOTE / 001

A choice of products is not the whole field of choice.

The ad sells individuality while keeping the decision inside the shop. Ask who sets the options, who profits, and what time, money, access, or support would make a different action feasible.

Possible next move: change the arrangement with other people—not merely the brand.

03 / OUR METHOD

Cut up the story.
Keep the evidence.

01 / INTERRUPT

Make the familiar look strange.

Put the slogan beside the receipt. Move the hero out of the frame. A cut-up can expose a hidden assumption; it cannot, by itself, prove a theory.

02 / TRACE

Follow the conditions.

Who owns the channel? Who sets the rule? Whose labor keeps the picture running? Sometimes the culprit is an ordinary incentive wearing a very expensive suit.

03 / ACT

Find room to move. Widen it.

Look for an action that changes what is possible: obtain a resource, refuse a role, build an alliance, contest a rule. Insight is a lead. Agency needs somewhere to go.

AGENCY POLICY: A coincidence is a clue to examine, not a verdict to announce.

A note from the Department of Context

A fictional, satirical agency, inspired by the existential detectives of I Heart Huckabees, William S. Burroughs’s cut-up sensibility, and the reality-disrupting humor of Robert Shea and Robert Anton Wilson’s The Illuminatus! Trilogy. This is an independent creative project, not an affiliated detective service.

Our working idea of agency includes the social and material conditions that enable action, individually and together. For background, see Hvinden and Halvorsen on agency, structure, and capabilities. For the satirical tradition, see Douglas Rushkoff on Operation Mindfuck.
